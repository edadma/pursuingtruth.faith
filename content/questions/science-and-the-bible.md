The short answer is: *no, but it depends what you mean by both
science and the Bible.*

The longer answer is what most of the rest of this site is for. Let
me try to put the shape of it on one page.

## What "science contradicts the Bible" usually means

When people say this, they almost always mean one of three things:

1. *The Bible says the earth is 6,000 years old, and science says
   it's 4.5 billion.*
2. *The Bible says the universe was created in six days, and science
   says it took 13.8 billion years.*
3. *Evolution disproves Genesis.*

These are all real conversations to have. None of them, in my view,
amounts to *science has refuted Christianity*. Let me explain why.

## On the age of the universe and the earth

The Christian tradition has, for most of its history, *not* been
committed to a young earth. Augustine, in the early fifth century,
read the days of Genesis 1 as a literary structure rather than a
strict chronology, and he had never heard of geology.[^augustine]
The young-earth reading is a particular interpretation of Genesis
that became culturally dominant only in the last century or two, and
it is not the only orthodox reading.

The standard ancient cosmological evidence — the age of the universe,
the age of the earth — does, in my reading, point clearly to deep
time. The fine-tuning of the constants ([fine-
tuning](/origins/the-fine-tuning-of-the-universe/)) is one of the
strongest *arguments for design* that the modern science has handed
us. The age of the universe is, if anything, *part of the Christian
case for God*, not against it.

## On Genesis 1 specifically

There is a tradition of careful biblical scholarship — going back at
least to Augustine and including many evangelical Old Testament
scholars today — that reads Genesis 1 as an *ancient near-Eastern
cosmology* with a profoundly theological purpose: to declare that
the *one* God, not the many gods of Israel's neighbors, made and
orders everything. The literary structure is striking: three days of
*forming* (light, sky/sea, land/plants) matched to three days of
*filling* (sun and moon, fish and birds, animals and humans). It
reads as poetry doing theological work.

You can debate the details of this reading. What I want to say is
that *taking Genesis 1 seriously* does not necessarily mean taking
it as a modern scientific timeline. The text was not given to a
modern audience, in modern scientific language, with modern
scientific questions in mind. It was given to ancient people in
ancient language, and what it teaches them is profoundly true —
*one God, not many; made on purpose, not by accident; you and I
made in his image, not as accidents*. That theology survives, intact,
the discovery that the universe is 13.8 billion years old.

## On evolution

Evolution is a more interesting case. The *fact* of common descent —
that life on earth shares a common ancestral history — is supported
by an enormous amount of converging evidence. The *mechanism* of
random mutation plus natural selection is well-documented to do real
work on a small scale. Where I am less convinced, and where I think
the honest scientific picture is also less convinced than the public
picture suggests, is in the claim that random mutation and natural
selection can account, by themselves, for the *information* in the
cell ([information in the cell](/origins/information-in-the-cell/))
and for the major transitions in the history of life.

There is, in short, real scientific debate here. The popular picture
of *evolution explains everything* is, in my reading, a confidence
that goes beyond the actual state of the science.

A Christian can hold a range of positions on evolution — from young-
earth creationism through old-earth creationism through intelligent
design through theistic evolution — and remain a serious Christian.
What no Christian should do is be intimidated into thinking the
science has settled the question against God. It hasn't.

## What I would say briefly

If you came in with the worry that *science has disproved
Christianity*, my short answer is:

- Science is the careful study of the world God made. There is no
  reason in principle to expect it to contradict the truth about that
  world.
- Many of the actual scientific results of the last hundred years —
  the Big Bang, fine-tuning, the information-rich structure of the
  cell — fit the theistic picture *better* than they fit the
  alternatives.
- The places where there is genuine apparent tension are usually
  places where a *particular interpretation* of the Bible is in
  tension with a *particular interpretation* of the science, and
  where the right move is to look harder at both.
